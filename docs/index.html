Home and C&I energy management on 15-minute Polish prices

A Polish prosumer with rooftop PV, a battery, a heat pump and an EV charger sees two price signals at once: exports are credited to a 12-month FIFO deposit at the 15-minute market price RCE (floored at zero when RCE is negative), while imports pay a flat, two-zone or dynamic tariff plus cash distribution fees. A commercial site on B23 faces a second rule: on each working day the capacity fee (141.20 PLN/MWh until December 2025, 219.40 PLN/MWh in 2026, charged on energy drawn 07:00-21:59) is multiplied by 0.17 when mean import in those hours stays within 5 % of mean import in the other hours (group K1). This project compares simple rules with a mixed-integer program solved once per day over the first full year of 15-minute prices (October 2025 to September 2026), runs the schedule against a localhost Modbus TCP inverter emulator with a watchdog, and exports the resulting entity states for Home Assistant. Code and method: github.com/kwwaleszczynski-del/pl-hems-mpc.

Household and C&I loads are synthetic. Energy prices and household distribution rates are illustrative round numbers; the B23 per-MWh charges are those of the TAURON Dystrybucja 2026 tariff, applied to the whole window. The net-billing and capacity-fee rules are simplified readings of the statutes (sources in the README) that must be verified before any real use. The Modbus register map belongs to the emulator only and must not be written to a real inverter.

1. Prosumer under 12-month FIFO net-billing

Annual settlement of a household with a 10 kWp PV array, a 10 kWh / 5 kW LFP battery, a heat pump with a one-state floor-slab heat store and an 11 kW EV charger over 35,040 quarter-hours (1 October 2025 to 30 September 2026). The deposit offsets active energy only; distribution fees are paid in cash, and at most 30 % of a month's credit still unused after 12 months is refunded.

The noisy-forecast MILP plans each day at 00:00 on actual PV and base load multiplied by independent N(1, 0.12) and N(1, 0.14) noise per quarter-hour and on 0.92 x SDAC as the RCE forecast; heat demand, COP and the EV schedule are taken as known. This is a synthetic forecast, not a forecasting model, so its gap to perfect foresight is optimistic.

3. Commercial site on B23 and the capacity fee (Art. 70a)

A 50 kWp PV + 100 kWh / 50 kW battery site on B23 with a 40 kW contracted power and a synthetic day-shift load. The ratio rule is causal: it charges from the grid between 00:00 and 06:59, keeps import in the fee hours at or below 1.04 times the mean import of 00:00-06:59 and lifts import after 22:00 to that same mean. The MILP solves each day with perfect foresight and chooses the K-group as part of the optimum, so it is an upper bound.

4. Closed-loop Modbus TCP test and watchdog

The schedule is replayed over a localhost TCP socket (function codes 0x03, 0x06, 0x10, unit ID 1) against an emulated inverter. On 1 May 2026 the EMS sends no writes at 12:00, 12:15 and 12:30. The emulator checks the heartbeat on register 40004 once per quarter-hour; when more than 900 s (register 40005) have passed without a change it sets 40016 = 1 and falls back from forced dispatch (mode 2) to self-consumption (mode 0) until writes resume. The fallback also lifts the export limit, so in the two tripped steps the emulator exports PV at an RCE below -1,000 PLN/MWh.

5. Home Assistant export

The repository exports MQTT discovery payloads and a per-quarter-hour state message derived from the noisy-forecast MILP dispatch, plus a configuration.yaml snippet for the emulator's register map. It is an export of entity definitions, not an installable integration, and it has not been tested against a running Home Assistant. Use the slider to step through the 96 quarter-hours of 1 May 2026.
