Home and C&I energy management on 15-minute Polish prices

A Polish prosumer with rooftop PV, a battery, a heat pump and an EV charger sees two price signals at once: exports are credited to a 12-month FIFO deposit at the 15-minute market price RCE (floored at zero when RCE is negative), while imports pay a flat, two-zone or dynamic tariff plus cash distribution fees. A commercial site on B23 faces a second non-linearity: the PSE capacity fee (141.20 PLN/MWh in peak hours) drops by 83 % on days when peak import stays within 5 % of off-peak import (band K1). This project tests how much a 15-minute mixed-integer controller saves over the first full year of 15-minute prices (October 2025 to September 2026), checks the schedule on a localhost Modbus TCP inverter emulator with a 15-minute watchdog, and packages the entities and register maps for Home Assistant. Code and method: github.com/kwwaleszczynski-del/pl-hems-mpc.

Tariff rates, distribution fees and inverter register addresses in this repository are illustrative configurations compiled from public tariff sheets and manuals, not billing advice or certified wiring instructions. Always check register tables and firmware notes against the manufacturer's manual before writing to a live inverter.

1. Prosumer under 12-month FIFO net-billing

Annual settlement of a household with a 10 kWp PV array, a 10 kWh / 5 kW LFP battery, a floor-slab heat pump (2R2C thermal model) and an 11 kW EV charger over 35,040 quarter-hours (1 October 2025 to 30 September 2026). The deposit offsets active energy only; distribution fees are paid in cash, and at most 20 % of any unused deposit from a given month can be refunded after 12 months.

Realizable MILP plans on a noisy 24-hour forecast (12 % PV, 14 % load error) and settles on actual prices and weather. Perfect foresight solves on actuals as an upper bound.

2. Dispatch in the negative-price week (1-3 May 2026)

On 1 May 2026 RCE fell to -1,458 PLN/MWh around solar noon. An uncontrolled battery fills up by 09:30 and exports the rest of the day at zero credit. The MILP controller holds battery headroom for the negative-price hours, pre-heats the floor slab and charges the EV around midday, curtailing only what exceeds the local load and battery limit.

3. Commercial site on B23 and the PSE capacity fee (Art. 70a)

A 50 kWp PV + 100 kWh / 50 kW battery site on B23 with a 40 kW connection limit. Under Art. 70a of the Capacity Market Act, the capacity fee (141.20 PLN/MWh across 07:00-21:59 on working days) is multiplied by A = 0.17 when the ratio of mean peak import to mean off-peak import stays within 5 % (band K1). Standard self-consumption discharges early in the afternoon and leaves the evening peak unbuffered; the MILP controller flattens the peak/off-peak ratio and places 87 % of working days in K1.

4. Closed-loop Modbus TCP test and hardware watchdog

The schedule is tested over a localhost TCP socket (function codes 0x03, 0x06, 0x10, unit ID 1) against a virtual inverter. On 1 May 2026 between 12:00 and 12:45 the EMS stops sending heartbeat increments on register 40004; after 900 s (register 40005) the inverter sets 40016 = 1 and falls back from forced dispatch (mode 2) to local self-consumption (mode 0) until writes resume.
